## Setup

The first time anyone opens a freshly deployed instance, OSSPlay redirects every request to `/setup`.
This page creates exactly one thing: the **instance root** account — the name, email, and password
you'll use to log in from now on. There's no organization or storage configuration here; it exists
purely to get one authenticated account into an otherwise-empty instance.

Once submitted, you're logged in immediately and redirected into onboarding. `/setup` itself becomes
permanently unreachable after this — visiting it again once a root account exists is rejected.

## Onboarding

Onboarding is a short, three-step wizard that runs once, right after setup, before you can reach the
dashboard. Each step is its own page (not a single scrolling form), so you can leave and come back to
exactly where you were:

1. **Domain** — point a domain at this server for automatic HTTPS. Skippable.
2. **Email** — configure SMTP so the instance can send invitation and password-reset emails.
   Skippable.
3. **Organization** — create your first organization. **Required** — an instance isn't useful for
   anything until at least one organization exists, so this step can't be skipped.

Skipping the domain or email step doesn't lose anything — both live under Instance settings (below)
and can be filled in later exactly the same way. There's no "you missed your chance" state; a skipped
step just shows as incomplete until you go fill it in from settings instead.

Until the required organization step is finished, every part of the dashboard redirects back into the
onboarding wizard — there's no way to end up on a dashboard screen with zero organizations to work in.

## Instance settings

Everything from the onboarding wizard's domain and email steps is also reachable later, as an
instance root, from the **Instance** section of the dashboard (**Domain** and **Email & SMTP**).
Filling either one in from there marks the corresponding onboarding step as complete too — there's a
single source of truth for "is this configured," not two separate settings that can drift out of
sync. The same section also covers [remote servers and SSH keys](/guides/remote-servers),
[instance users](/guides/account-recovery#if-youre-an-instance-root-helping-someone-else), and the
[audit log](/guides/audit-logs).

## If you get locked out

See [Account recovery](/guides/account-recovery) for what to do if you can't log back in —
including the case where the instance root itself has no working password and no working second
factor.
